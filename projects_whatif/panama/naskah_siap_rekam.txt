What if the world's most important shortcut ran out of water?

This is the Panama Canal. About 80 kilometers of locks and lakes that save ships from sailing all the way around South America.

And this year, it had its best year ever. When war choked the Strait of Hormuz, Asia started buying American oil and gas. Those tankers squeezed through Panama, and the canal's revenue hit a record 6.6 billion dollars.

But here's the problem. This canal doesn't run on fuel. It runs on rain.

Every ship that crosses can use around 50 million gallons of fresh water from Gatun Lake. And that same lake gives drinking water to about half of Panama.

Now El Niño is back. The canal has already cut how many ships can pass each day, and the dry season could start a month early.

So if the rain doesn't come, Panama has to choose: water for ships, or water for people.

And by law, people come first.
