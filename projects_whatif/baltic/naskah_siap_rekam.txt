What if Russia cut every cable under the Baltic Sea?

The Baltic is shallow, only about fifty-five meters deep. Across its floor run dozens of cables and pipelines, carrying power, gas, and internet between Finland, Sweden, Germany, and the Baltic states.

And they keep breaking.

In 2023, a ship's anchor ripped open a gas pipeline between Finland and Estonia. On Christmas Day 2024, the tanker Eagle S, part of Russia's shadow fleet, dragged its anchor for nearly a hundred kilometers and cut a power cable to Estonia. On New Year's Eve 2025, Finland seized another ship with drag marks leading to a broken cable.

Every time, it's hard to prove it wasn't an accident.

In 2025, Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania unplugged from Russia's power grid and joined Europe's. Part of that link runs under this sea.

So NATO now patrols here, and the US just sent Marines with drones to watch the seabed.

But you can't guard every meter of cable. One dragging anchor is all it takes.
