What if the world's most important oil route was only 54 kilometers wide?

That's the Strait of Hormuz. Every day, about 20 million barrels of oil squeeze through here. That's one fifth of all the oil the world uses. And each direction gets a shipping lane just two miles wide.

So why not just go around it?

Because there is no around. For Kuwait, Qatar, Iraq and Bahrain, this is the only way out to the open sea.

Saudi Arabia and the UAE built pipelines to skip it. But together, they can move only a few million barrels a day. Even Iran built one. It barely gets used.

And here's the twist. Most of this oil isn't going to America. About 84 percent goes to Asia, led by China, India, Japan and South Korea.

Nearly a fifth of the world's LNG uses the same door.

As of November 2026, traffic here has dropped to a fraction of normal. And the whole world is watching one narrow gap.
